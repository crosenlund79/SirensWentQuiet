# Version A: True 3D Voxels

Scene: `res://src/voxel/voxel_demo.tscn` (launcher key **A**). Code: `src/voxel/`. Template art: `assets/graphics/voxel/`, generated by `tools/art/voxel/make_voxel_art.py`. Tests: `tests/voxel/`.

The whole house (floors, walls, window frames, glass, trim and every piece of furniture) is one dense volume of **0.1 m voxels** built from `HousePlan`. Nothing in the house is a hand-modelled mesh. Every shot removes real voxels: the wall's thickness, its layers and the room behind it appear through the hole, and the collision and navigation follow.

## How to play and what to try

WASD move, mouse aim, hold LMB to shoot, mouse wheel to zoom (11–32 m); **N** day/night, **R** reset, **Esc** launcher, **H** help, **F3** stats (shared `DemoHud`).

- **Chew through a wall.** Shoot the same spot on an exterior wall. The first shots peel the painted lap siding (outside) or the room paint (inside) and expose pink insulation and pine studs. About 12 shots in a 3 × 4 pattern open a walk-through hole. Walk through it, and watch psychos use it.
- **Look at thickness from above.** Shoot near the top of a wall: the dark wall cap breaks open and shows the three layers in section.
- **Shoot through a hole.** Bullets pass through openings you made, carry on and hit whatever is behind.
- **Windows.** The first hit shatters the whole pane into glass chips; the sill and frame then take damage like a wall.
- **Interior walls.** Each side is painted for its own room, so a hole between the living room and the bedroom shows greige on one side and sage on the other, with studs between.
- **Tall furniture** (wardrobe, fridge, bookshelf, shelves, water heater, floor lamp, hood) stops bullets and gets carved too. Low furniture is shot over, per the SPEC.
- **Floors** take shallow craters (planks, tiles, concrete); the foundation row never goes.
- **Rubble.** Chips fly in the colours of the voxels they came from, bounce off the voxel grid and settle into small rubble piles that fade after about 10 s.
- **Enemies bash walls.** Put yourself in the bedroom with psychos outside the north wall: they chew a body-wide hole in about 15–20 s with two or three of them.
- **Night.** Moonlit blue with warm practicals (floor lamp, kitchen hood, bedside lamp, the flickering bathroom ceiling light, the utility bulb), the weapon light, a faint self light and warm glow on lamp shades. Light spills through windows and through the holes you shoot.
- **Cutaway.** Voxels between the camera and the player are cut away voxel by voxel above 0.45 m, leaving wall stubs with dark section caps. The roof dissolves while you are inside but still casts its shadow, so sunlight enters only through windows and holes. Characters hidden behind walls show a faint x-ray silhouette (warm for the player, pale red for psychos).

Screenshot scenarios (no extra gameplay; they place the player, fire real shots, spawn enemies and frame the camera): `--scenario=overview | blasted | layers | interior | shooting | enemies | firefight`, plus `--night`. Some freeze the frame (tree pause) so short-lived effects are visible to the slow software renderer.


## Technical approach

| Module | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `voxel_grid.gd` (`VoxelGrid`) | Dense volume: material byte, body colour, paint colour and a per-face paint mask per cell; empty 1-cell border; Amanatides–Woo ray traversal; column queries |
| `voxel_mat.gd` (`VoxelMat`) | Material ids and per-id tables: solid, structural (wall), glass, shot-through, indestructible, emissive, glossy, hardness, impact effect |
| `voxel_house.gd` (`VoxelHouse`) | Builds `HousePlan` into the grid: floors, layered walls, openings, trim, baseboards |
| `voxel_furniture.gd` (`VoxelFurniture`) | 26 furniture types and light fixtures as small implicit functions evaluated per voxel |
| `voxel_mesher.gd` (`VoxelMesher`) | Face-culled mesher with baked per-vertex AO; opaque and glass surfaces; collision triangles |
| `voxel_world.gd` (`VoxelWorld`) | 16³ chunks: one `MeshInstance3D` + one `StaticBody3D` (`ConcavePolygonShape3D`, layer *walls*) each; dirty-chunk rebuilds |
| `voxel_carver.gd` (`VoxelCarver`) | Carving, glass shattering, crumble rules, `GridNav` synchronisation (pure data, tested headless) |
| `voxel_demo.gd` | Scene root: aiming, hitscan, enemies, cutaway, HUD stats, SPEC debug API |
| `voxel_rig.gd`, `voxel_player.gd`, `voxel_enemy.gd` | 0.05 m voxel-sculpted characters with procedural gait, aim, recoil, swing, hit flash, collapse |
| `voxel_fx.gd` | Pooled `MultiMesh` debris (simulated against the voxel grid), dust puffs, sparks; tracers; muzzle flash |
| `voxel_yard.gd`, `voxel_lighting.gd`, `voxel_camera.gd`, `voxel_scenarios.gd` | Ground, props, grass, roof; day/night; SPEC camera; screenshot setups |

**Grid and plan alignment.** Voxel centres lie on multiples of 0.1 m in plan X/Y (grid origin −0.45, −0.4, −0.45; 149 × 31 × 99 cells). Every plan rect maps to voxels by the same *centre in [start, end)* rule that `GridNav` uses, so walls, doors, windows and furniture line up with the navigation raster exactly. The exterior walls are exactly 0.3 m (3 voxels) and centred. Interior walls are 0.2 m (2 voxels), offset 5 cm from the centre line. A test checks that every WALL nav cell has wall voxels and that no OPEN cell has any (apart from door jambs).

**Walls.** Exterior walls are built from three layers. The outside skin is painted lap siding (pattern from `voxel/siding`, 2-voxel boards with a shadow lip) on raw pine, with a concrete plinth below 0.2 m and dark corner boards. The core has studs every 0.6 m, bottom and double top plates, and insulation between them. The inside skin is drywall painted for its room, or bathroom tiles up to 1.2 m, or the kitchen's sage subway backsplash. Each voxel has a *body* colour and a *paint* colour applied only to its finished faces (a per-face mask). Fresh cuts therefore show raw wood, gypsum or tile biscuit, while intact surfaces keep paint and glaze. Wall tops use a dark cap colour, so from above the floor plan reads like an architectural drawing. Windows have an aluminium frame ring, a mullion and glass in the middle layer, casings on both skins and protruding sills. Doors have trim jambs, a header and a threshold.

**Meshing.** Each exposed face becomes its own quad (no greedy merging, so per-voxel colour survives). COLOR.rgb holds the face colour and COLOR.a the AO term. UV carries emissive, glossy and finished-face flags. The shader feeds AO into `AO` (`AO_LIGHT_AFFECT` 0.45), converts the sRGB vertex colours to linear, and adds a soft voxel bevel only on raw cuts, furniture and glossy finishes, so intact paint stays smooth and the voxel grain shows where damage reveals it. Glass is a separate transparent surface. About 75,000 faces (300,000 vertices) in about 114 chunks.

**Destruction.** Bullets use a voxel ray traversal (exact hit voxel; low furniture is shot through), plus a physics ray for enemies and props. A wall hit carves a ragged crater of radius about 0.23 m: noise per voxel, scaled by material hardness, and stretched 1.5× along the shot while tapering with depth, so the layers show as rings. A hit on glass shatters the face-connected pane. After every carve a small *crumble* pass settles the damage, repeated around whatever fell (at most three rounds):

- loose crumbs with at most one neighbour fall
- free-hanging horizontal strips at most two voxels tall fall
- free-standing one-voxel posts topple
- wall stubs at most two voxels tall at floor level crumble, so holes reach a 0.1 m sill
- small components that a bounded flood fill (400 voxels) shows to be detached from the house fall

Everything removed becomes debris in its own colour. These rules are local and bounded; there is **no structural integrity simulation** (out of scope).

**Collision and navigation (SPEC §1).** In the same call as the carve, the touched chunks rebuild their collision: triangles of structural voxels in the rows overlapping 0.1–1.6 m only, since bodies are 0.14–1.56 m cylinders, so a sill below 0.15 m doesn't block them. `VoxelCarver.sync_nav` then re-tests each WALL cell in the changed area and calls `nav.refresh_region`. Meshes rebuild within a 6 ms per-frame budget, normally in the same frame. *Deviation, on purpose:* the SPEC's point test at the cell centre misses most of a 3-voxel wall (the centre column only sees the middle layer, so a cell could open while the siding still blocks). A cell becomes OPEN when no wall voxel remains between 0.15 m and 1.6 m in **any voxel column whose centre lies in the cell's 0.25 m footprint**. On the intact house this gives exactly the shared raster, it never reopens a wall, and it never opens nav over remaining collision.

**Enemy bites.** On `swing == "wall"`, the bite aims at a random voxel that still blocks the body-wide passage around `r.target` (the target cell plus one cell either side along the wall, between 0.2 and 1.55 m). If there is none, it falls back to the SPEC's random height of 0.2–1.5 m. Radius 0.17 m. Measured: two or three psychos get through an exterior wall in about 16–21 s; one alone takes about 40 s.

**Characters.** The figures are sculpted at 0.05 m from implicit shapes: torso, head, arms and legs pivot separately, and a gun is held in the hand. The player wears a dark jacket, an orange beanie and scarf (the one warm accent), a brown strap and an olive backpack; they aim with both hands and the arms kick on recoil. Psychos come in four looks: pale skin, torn everyday clothes with skin showing through, stains, a hunch, a limp, and arms hanging forward. A shared shader adds a rim light (stronger at night), a white hit flash and a dithered fade for corpses, and an x-ray pass where walls hide the figure.

**Lighting.** Forward+: a directional sun/moon with 4-split soft shadows, SSAO, soft-light glow, exponential fog, filmic tonemap and a slight desaturation. Day is overcast late afternoon with low warm sun from the west-southwest and all fixtures off. Night is a blue moon with the `HousePlan.LIGHTS` marked `night_on` as shadowed omni lights; the bathroom bulb flickers via summed sines with occasional drop-outs. Emissive lamp-shade voxels glow at night. The transition tweens over 0.5 s.

**Yard and roof.** The ground is a generated texture with one texel per 0.1 m (nearest filtering), so it matches the voxel scale. It includes a dead lawn from `voxel/ground`, concrete slabs, the driveway with an oil stain, the sidewalk, asphalt with faded dashes and skid marks, a gravel strip along the house, and leaf litter under the trees. Voxel props: a maroon sedan with a flat tyre and a smashed window, three dead trees, a tipped bin with spilled bags, a mailbox, a dead streetlight, the porch deck and the back step. About 2,600 dry grass tufts. The flat voxel roof has a coping, an AC unit, vents and a skylight, and dissolves voxel by voxel when you enter.

## Replaceable art (logical names)

All images load through `AssetResolver.get_texture("voxel/<name>")`. Put `custom/graphics/voxel/<name>.png` next to the project or next to an exported Windows/Linux game to override; it takes effect after a restart. Swatches are **one pixel = one voxel face**, any size; pixel art fits naturally, and so does a drawing app such as Doodle Nook. Floors and siding tile; rugs stretch to their rug.

Palette slots, left to right, top to bottom: **row 0** cut siding wood, exterior trim, window frames, plinth, wall caps, thresholds, roof (unused), fascia · **row 1** paint for the bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, living room and utility room, gypsum, interior trim, baseboards · **row 2** studs, screed, foundation, glass tint, bathroom tile, kitchen tile, tile biscuit, exterior sills · **row 3** sofa, armchair, duvet, kitchen cabinets, worktop, dark wood, light wood, dark metal.

Furniture details, characters and props are procedural (colours in code). The shared crosshair is `common/crosshair`.

## Performance

Measured headless on the shared 4-core VM (GDScript, other agents running; a desktop CPU is typically faster). The HUD (F3) shows the live numbers.

- **House:** 100,514 voxels; 114 non-empty 16³ chunks (of 140); 75,044 faces, 300,176 vertices, 147 surfaces (opaque + glass).
- **Startup:** building the grid from `HousePlan` takes about 470 ms, and meshing plus collision for every chunk about 200–240 ms. **R** copies a cached pristine grid (2.5 ms, copy-on-write arrays) instead of rebuilding; a full reload (yard, roof, props, characters) takes about 0.8 s.
- **Per wall hit** (40 hits on one wall), see the table below.
- **Draw calls** (lavapipe, 1600×900): about 370–480 by day and about 650–770 at night (shadowed practicals and the weapon light). Software rendering runs at about 1 fps; judge by these counts and timings, not that FPS.
- **No node per voxel**, no rigid bodies: debris uses 3 `MultiMesh`es (700 cubes, 160 puffs, 140 sparks) simulated in GDScript against the voxel grid.

| Per wall hit | Average | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Carve + crumble pass | 2.4–4.9 ms | 5.8–7.4 ms |
| Collision rebuild (same call) | 0.6–1.0 ms | 3–4 ms |
| Mesh rebuild (6 ms/frame budget) | 2.4–3.7 ms over about 2.3–3.4 chunks | 6.5–10 ms |
| One chunk's mesh | about 1.5 ms | 4–6.4 ms (furniture-dense chunks) |

## Known issues and limitations

- **GDScript cost.** A shot costs a few milliseconds of carving and meshing. That's fine for one gun, but a shotgun, explosions or many simultaneous enemy bites would need threaded meshing (`WorkerThreadPool`) or a native mesher. Initial build takes about 0.7 s.
- **Many draw calls.** With 114 chunks plus shadow passes there are 360–650 draw calls. Merging chunk columns or using larger chunks would cut this at some cost in rebuild time.
- **Crumble rules are heuristics.** They keep holes clean and walkable and avoid floating crumbs. They are not physics: a large detached slab (over 400 voxels) can still hang in the air, since no structural integrity was asked for.
- **0.1 m resolution.** Furniture is chunky (chair legs are 10 cm), and a single voxel is about 3–6 px at the default zoom, so fine damage is best seen zoomed in. Halving the voxel size would multiply the cost by 8.
- **Cutaway.** The cut follows a cylinder toward the camera, so walls beside the player can be cut too. Stubs show flat dark caps rather than their true layers.
- **Enemy unstick swing.** Holes open cell by cell, so a psycho can stand in a half-open gap where the shared `PursuitBrain`'s path is OPEN but not walkable. The brain then oscillates instead of reporting "stuck". `VoxelEnemy` adds one rule: after 2.5 s without getting closer to the player, it swings at the nearest remaining WALL cell within reach. Without it, a lone psycho can stall forever in a narrow hole.
- **Furniture collision** is a box per plan rect on *low_obstacles* (layer 5), and bullets interact with furniture voxels instead. Carving a tall piece doesn't shrink its box.
- **Screenshots.** Under lavapipe the scene clock barely advances before `tools/capture.sh`'s timer fires, so short effects and roof transitions are missed. The fixed-fps command above gives deterministic frames.
